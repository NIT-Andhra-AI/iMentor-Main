### Chapter 2: Motion

#### Definitions and Concepts

1. **Distance**: The total length of the path travelled by an object, irrespective of its direction.
   - **Unit**: Meter (m)

2. **Displacement**: The shortest distance between the initial and final positions of an object, along with the direction.
   - **Unit**: Meter (m)

3. **Speed**: The rate at which distance is covered, without considering the direction.
   - **Formula**: \( \text{Speed} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}} \)
   - **Unit**: Meter per second (m/s)

4. **Velocity**: The rate of change of displacement with time, including both magnitude and direction.
   - **Formula**: \( \text{Velocity} = \frac{\text{Displacement}}{\text{Time}} \)
   - **Unit**: Meter per second (m/s)

5. **Uniform Motion**: When an object travels in a straight line with constant speed, covering equal distances in equal intervals of time.
   - **Example**: A car moving at a constant 60 km/h on a straight highway.

6. **Non-Uniform Motion**: When an object travels with variable speed or changes its path.
   - **Example**: A car accelerating from a stoplight or turning corners.

7. **Acceleration**: The rate of change of velocity with time, indicating how quickly the velocity of an object changes.
   - **Formula**: \( \text{Acceleration} = \frac{\text{Change in Velocity}}{\text{Time}} = \frac{v - u}{t} \)
   - **Unit**: Meter per second squared (m/s²)

8. **Equations of Motion**: Three fundamental equations derived from the basic definitions of velocity and acceleration.

   - **First Equation of Motion**: \( v = u + at \)
     - Where \( v \) is the final velocity, \( u \) is the initial velocity, \( a \) is the acceleration, and \( t \) is the time.

   - **Second Equation of Motion**: \( s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2 \)
     - Where \( s \) is the displacement, \( u \) is the initial velocity, \( a \) is the acceleration, and \( t \) is the time.

   - **Third Equation of Motion**: \( v^2 = u^2 + 2as \)
     - Where \( v \) is the final velocity, \( u \) is the initial velocity, \( a \) is the acceleration, and \( s \) is the displacement.

#### Summary of Concepts and Formulae

- **Distance**: Total path length (m)
- **Displacement**: Shortest path with direction (m)
- **Speed**: \( \text{Speed} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}} \) (m/s)
- **Velocity**: \( \text{Velocity} = \frac{\text{Displacement}}{\text{Time}} \) (m/s)
- **Uniform Motion**: Constant speed in a straight line
- **Non-Uniform Motion**: Variable speed or changing path
- **Acceleration**: \( \text{Acceleration} = \frac{\text{Change in Velocity}}{\text{Time}} \) (m/s²)
- **Equations of Motion**:
  - \( v = u + at \)
  - \( s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2 \)
  - \( v^2 = u^2 + 2as \)

These notes provide a structured overview of the key concepts and formulae for Chapter 2: Motion. Understanding these definitions and equations will help in solving various problems related to motion.

### Higher Order Thinking Question (HOTS) for Circular Motion and Vertical Stone Throws

#### Question
Consider a stone thrown vertically upwards with an initial velocity \( v_0 \). At the highest point of its trajectory, the stone momentarily comes to a stop. Discuss the following:

1. **Velocity and Acceleration at the Highest Point:**
   - Explain why the velocity of the stone is zero at the highest point.
   - Describe the acceleration of the stone at this point and explain why it is constant.

2. **Comparison with Circular Motion:**
   - Compare the acceleration at the highest point of the stone's trajectory with the centripetal acceleration in circular motion.
   - Explain the similarities and differences in the nature of these accelerations.

3. **Implications for the Stone's Motion:**
   - Discuss the implications of the stone's velocity and acceleration at the highest point on its overall motion.
   - Explain how these conditions affect the stone's descent.

#### Detailed Explanation

1. **Velocity and Acceleration at the Highest Point:**

   - **Velocity:**
     At the highest point of the stone's trajectory, the stone momentarily stops moving upward. This means its vertical velocity becomes zero at that instant. The reason for this is that the net force acting on the stone is gravity, which always acts downward. As the stone rises, gravity slows it down until it stops and then starts to pull it back downward.

     \[
     \text{At highest point, } v = 0
     \]

   - **Acceleration:**
     The acceleration of the stone at the highest point is due to gravity, which is a constant force. The magnitude of this acceleration is \( g \) (approximately \( 9.8 \, \text{m/s}^2 \)), and it always acts downward, regardless of the stone's position in its trajectory.

     \[
     \text{At highest point, } a = g = 9.8 \, \text{m/s}^2 \, \text{(downward)}
     \]

2. **Comparison with Circular Motion:**

   - **Centripetal Acceleration:**
     In circular motion, the object moves in a circular path at a constant speed. The acceleration in this case is always directed towards the center of the circle and is called centripetal acceleration. The magnitude of the centripetal acceleration is given by:

     \[
     a_c = \frac{v^2}{r}
     \]

     where \( v \) is the constant speed and \( r \) is the radius of the circular path.

   - **Similarities and Differences:**
     - **Similarity:** Both the stone and the object in circular motion experience acceleration that is always directed towards a fixed point (the center of the circle or the highest point of the stone's trajectory).
     - **Difference:** The stone's acceleration at the highest point is constant and always downward, whereas the centripetal acceleration in circular motion is always directed towards the center of the circle and depends on the speed and radius of the circular path.

3. **Implications for the Stone's Motion:**

   - **Velocity and Acceleration at the Highest Point:**
     At the highest point, the velocity is zero, and the acceleration is constant and downward. This means that the stone is momentarily in a state of rest, but it is still being pulled downward by gravity. This condition is crucial because it marks the transition from the upward phase to the downward phase of the stone's trajectory.

   - **Overall Motion:**
     The stone's descent is influenced by the same gravitational force that caused it to stop at the highest point. As the stone starts to fall, its velocity increases due to the constant downward acceleration. The stone's motion is a result of the interplay between its initial upward velocity and the constant downward acceleration due to gravity.

     \[
     \text{During descent, } a = g = 9.8 \, \text{m/s}^2 \, \text{(downward)}
     \]

     The stone's velocity during descent can be described by the equation of motion:

     \[
     v = u + at
     \]

     where \( u = 0 \) (at the highest point) and \( a = g \).

     \[
     v = gt
     \]

     This shows that the velocity increases linearly with time during the descent.

By understanding these concepts, you can better grasp the dynamics of both vertical stone throws and circular motion, and appreciate the underlying physics that governs these phenomena.
